10 Reasons Small Shops in Kenya Close in the First Year (and How to Avoid Each)

Most shops that close do not fail because customers stopped coming. They fail because money quietly leaks out through stock, credit and untracked costs. Here are 10 common causes and a simple fix for each.

The short answer

Shops close when owners cannot see where their money is going.

Almost every reason below comes down to missing records. Once you can see stock, credit, expenses and profit, most of these problems are caught early.

Opening a shop is exciting. You find a good location, stock the shelves and customers start walking in. Sales look healthy, yet a few months later the money is gone and you cannot say where it went.

This happens to many new shop owners, and it is rarely because of one big mistake. It is usually a handful of small leaks that nobody was watching.

The good news is that every leak below can be spotted and fixed early, if you keep simple, accurate records. That is exactly what a POS system like Ruo System is built to do.

As you read, note how many of these 10 could already be happening in your shop.
Reason 1

Confusing sales with profit

A busy shop feels successful. But sales is only the money that came in. Profit is what remains after stock, rent, wages, transport and every other cost.

The problem

You see a full till and assume the business is doing well, then cannot pay a supplier or rent at month end.

How to avoid it

Record what each item cost you and what you sold it for, then check profit weekly, not just sales.

Reason 2

Putting cash into the wrong stock

New owners often buy what looks good or what a supplier pushes, not what their customers actually buy.

The problem

Your money sits on shelves in slow items while the fast sellers run out and customers go elsewhere.

How to avoid it

Look at your best sellers and slow movers every week. Restock what sells and pause buying what does not.

Reason 3

Stock going missing without anyone noticing

Theft, wrong change, damaged goods and counting mistakes all reduce your stock. If you only count occasionally, you find the gap too late to learn what caused it.

The problem

At the end of the month the shelves hold less than they should, and you cannot say when or how it happened.

How to avoid it

Record every sale so stock reduces item by item. Compare the system count with the shelf often, so gaps show up in days, not months.

Reason 4

Giving credit and never collecting it

Credit keeps regular customers loyal, but unpaid credit is stock you will never be paid for.

The problem

Debts sit in a notebook. Names are forgotten, amounts are disputed and old balances are never mentioned.

How to avoid it

Tie every credit sale to the customer's name, set a limit for each person, and follow up on balances on a regular day each week.

Reason 5

Mixing shop money with personal money

It is easy to take cash from the till for school fees, airtime or a family emergency, especially when the shop is your main income.

The problem

Money leaves the shop with no record, so you cannot tell whether the business is growing or being drained.

How to avoid it

Pay yourself a fixed amount on a fixed day. Record anything else you take as a withdrawal so the shop's numbers stay honest.

Reason 6

Not tracking small expenses

Rent is easy to remember. Transport, electricity, packaging, licences and M-Pesa charges are not, yet together they can take a large part of your earnings.

The problem

You count the money from sales but forget the costs, so you feel richer than you are.

How to avoid it

Write down every expense on the day it happens and review the total each month against your profit.

Reason 7

Pricing without knowing your costs

Setting prices by copying the shop next door can leave you selling some items at almost no margin, or at a loss once other costs are added.

The problem

You sell a lot of an item and feel good about it, but each sale earns very little.

How to avoid it

Know the buying cost of each item, add your running costs, and set a price that leaves a real margin.

Reason 8

Running out of your best sellers

A customer who finds an empty shelf may not come back. Stockouts cost you sales you never see.

The problem

You only realise an item is finished when a customer asks for it.

How to avoid it

Keep a minimum level for your fastest items and reorder before you reach it, using real sales numbers instead of guesses.

Reason 9

Stock that expires or never sells

This hurts most in cosmetic shops, pharmacies and supermarkets. Expired or dead stock is money you cannot get back.

The problem

You discover expired items at the back of the shelf when it is too late to discount or return them.

How to avoid it

Buy smaller amounts of new items, check expiry dates regularly, and discount slow stock early to turn it back into cash.

Reason 10

Keeping records in a notebook or in your head

This is the reason behind many of the others. When records are incomplete, you only notice a problem after it has already cost you money.

The problem

Notebooks get lost, pages go missing and the evening is spent adding up instead of resting.

How to avoid it

Record each sale once, as it happens, in a system that updates stock, payments, credit and profit for you.

What These 10 Have in Common

Look back at the list. Nearly every reason is about not seeing something in time: profit, stock, credit, expenses or expiry. The shop was not doomed from the start. The owner just did not have the numbers early enough to act.

You do not need to fix everything at once. A practical way to begin:

  • Week 1: record every sale and every expense, even the small ones
  • Week 2: compare your stock records with the shelf and look into any gaps
  • Week 3: list who owes you and agree on how they will pay
  • Week 4: check your profit and best sellers, then adjust what you buy

A POS makes each of these steps faster because the numbers update as you sell.

Frequently asked questions

Why do small shops in Kenya close in the first year?

The most common causes are not knowing real profit, buying the wrong stock, losing stock unnoticed, unpaid customer credit, mixing shop and personal money, and untracked expenses. Most come from missing or incomplete records.

How can a new shop owner avoid failing?

Record every sale and expense, check stock against the shelf often, control customer credit, pay yourself a fixed amount, and review profit every week. A POS system can do much of this tracking for you.

Do I need a POS system for a small shop?

You can start with a notebook, but a POS saves time and catches problems earlier because stock, payments, credit and profit update as you sell. Ruo System costs KSh 2,000 per month and has a demo you can try first.

How often should I check my shop's numbers?

Check sales and cash daily, and review stock, credit, expenses and profit at least once a week. Small problems are far easier to fix when you catch them early.
