Christmas Stocking Guide for Kenyan Shops: How Much Stock to Buy in December 2026

Buy too little and customers walk out. Buy too much and your cash sits on the shelf in January. Here is a simple formula, five steps, and a free calculator.

December can be the best month of the year for a shop. It can also be the most expensive mistake. Overstock, and January rent, school fees and restocking bills arrive while your money is still sitting on the shelves.

Most owners stock for Christmas by feeling: "Last year we ran out of soda, so I will double it." Doubling everything is how cash gets stuck in slow products while fast sellers still run out. The fix is to plan with your own sales numbers. This guide shows how, then covers what to stock for Christmas in five types of shop.

Step 1

Find out what you sold last December

Compare last December with a normal month, such as September or October. For your top 20 to 30 products, write down the units sold per week. These products usually make most of your sales.

If you use a POS, run your sales-by-product report for both periods. With a notebook, start with your 20 best sellers. A new shop with no December history can use the last 4 to 8 weeks of sales instead.

Step 2

Work out your Christmas boost for each product

Your Christmas boost shows how much more a product sells in December.

Christmas boost = December weekly sales ÷ normal weekly sales

If you normally sell 120 sodas a week and sold 180 a week last December, your boost is 180 ÷ 120 = 1.5. No history yet? Start near 1.3 for fast-moving food and drinks and adjust after the first week. That is a starting guess, not a rule. Each product has its own boost, so do not use one number for the whole shop.

Step 3

Use this formula to decide how much to buy

Stock to buy = (normal weekly sales × Christmas boost × weeks) + safety stock − stock you already have

Weeks is how long you must cover before your next reliable delivery, usually 2 to 3. Safety stock is a buffer of about 10 to 20% so one busy weekend does not empty a shelf. Example numbers:

Step 4

Check your budget before you order

Multiply each "buy" number by your cost price and add up the total. Then ask two questions. Can I pay this without touching money for rent, wages and January restocking? Is too much cash going into one slow product?

If the total is too high, trim slow items first and protect your fast movers. Keep some cash aside to top up your best sellers in mid-December.

Step 5

Buy in stages, not in one big order

  • Early December: the bulk of your fast movers and non-perishables.
  • Mid December: a smaller top-up based on what actually sold in the first two weeks.
  • Around the 20th: only what you are sure will sell before the rush ends, such as last-minute gifts and travel items.

Staging keeps your cash flexible. Supplier stock and prices can move fast in December, so confirm availability with your regular suppliers early.

By shop type

What to Stock for Christmas in a Mini Supermarket in Kenya

Mini supermarkets and grocery shops gain from cooking, hosting and gifting. Focus your boost on products people buy for the table and for visitors.

  • DrinksSodas, juice and water. Plan fridge space early.
  • Cooking basicsOil, flour, sugar, rice, salt, spices
  • Tea, coffee and bread spreadsSteady sellers when guests visit
  • Snacks and sweetsStrong for impulse buying and gifts

Count your best-selling 30 products first, and keep slow items to small quantities.

By shop type

Christmas Stock Planning for Cosmetics and Beauty Shops in Kenya

Customers shop for gifts and for looking good over the holidays. Hair products, lotions, perfumes and gift sets usually lead. Bundles and small gift packs make it easier for customers to buy more than one item.

Perfume and extensions sell less often than shampoo, so give them a smaller share of your budget. Customer frequency matters more than margin alone. Our cosmetic shop startup budget guide shows how to split capital across categories.

By shop type

Christmas Stock for Pharmacies in Kenya: Fast Movers and Expiry Dates

Customers still need everyday medicine over the holidays, and many suppliers are slower to deliver, so keep your regular fast movers well stocked before the 20th.

Check expiry dates on everything you order, and avoid buying large quantities of slow items that could expire. Follow the rules that apply to your pharmacy for records and stock.

By shop type

Hardware Shop Stock in December in Kenya: What Sells Before and After Christmas

Many homeowners repair, paint and fix electrical items before guests arrive or before they travel. Paint, tools and small electrical items can rise, but demand is uneven, so do not over-order slow items.

Hardware shops sell by pieces, metres and bags, and often to customers on credit. Record both, because a shop can look busy and still have unpaid money outside.

By shop type

Bookshop and Stationery Stock in Kenya: Plan for January School Opening

For bookshops and stationery shops, Christmas is smaller and January is the big month. School opening brings exercise books, pens, geometry sets, uniforms and bags.

The risk is spending all your cash on December stock and having none left for January. Work out your January needs now with the same formula, and keep that money aside.

Common Christmas stocking mistakes

  • Doubling everything. Boost fast movers, not the whole shop.
  • Ignoring cash. Profit on paper does not pay January bills.
  • Buying new products in bulk. Test small quantities first.
  • Same stock all month. In many estates, people travel upcountry and sales slow after the main rush.
  • Wrong sales records. A wrong plan this year becomes a wrong plan next year.

What to do with leftover stock after Christmas

In early January, check your slow-moving report. Bundle slow items with fast ones, run a short discount on what you cannot hold, and do not reorder anything that did not sell. Write down this year's boost for each product so next year's plan takes five minutes.

Frequently asked questions

How much stock should I buy for Christmas?

Use this formula for each product: normal weekly sales × Christmas boost × weeks, plus 10 to 20% safety stock, minus what you already have.

When should I start stocking for Christmas?

Plan in November and buy your main fast-moving stock in early December. Top up in mid-December based on what actually sells.

What is a good Christmas boost to use?

Divide last December's weekly sales by a normal week's sales. With no history, start near 1.3 for fast-moving food and drinks and adjust after the first week.

How do I avoid overstocking for Christmas?

Plan your top 20 to 30 products with numbers, set a budget before you order, and buy in stages instead of one big order.

What should a bookshop stock in December?

Keep December stock modest and plan for January school opening, which is usually the bigger month for bookshops and stationery shops.
