You open at 8am. By lunch the shop is full: someone wants Vaseline, another asks for relaxer, a salon owner collects hair products in bulk. Then evening comes, you count everything, and the money does not match the day you just had.

Busy does not mean profitable. A cosmetic shop sells hundreds of small, fast-moving items on thin margins, so money leaks out quietly. These are the seven signs it is happening in your shop.

Sign 1

Stock Disappearing in Your Cosmetic Shop: Stock Theft and Leaks

You sold 20 items today, but the shelf looks like 30 are gone. Cosmetics are small, expensive for their size and easy to pocket: lip gloss, perfume, nail polish, small tubes of cream.

  • Items taken by staff or customers without payment
  • Testers and samples that are never written off
  • Breakages and spillages of lotions, oils and perfumes that nobody records
  • Sales that were never entered, so the count never matches

Quick test: count 10 fast movers today and again after a week. Compare against what you sold and restocked. If the numbers differ, you have a leak.

Sign 2

Wrong Pricing: How to Price Cosmetics and Protect Your Margin

Suppliers change prices, transport costs move, yet your shelf price stays the same for months. You may think a product earns 20% when it really earns 5%. Regular-customer discounts "just a little" can erase the profit completely.

Watch the wholesale trap too. When a salon owner buys 12 pieces at a price close to what you paid, check your margin before you agree.

Quick test: for your 10 best sellers, write down the last buying price and today's selling price. If you cannot find the buying price quickly, you are pricing blind.

Sign 3

Unpaid Credit: How to Manage Customer Debt in a Retail Shop

"Nitalipa kesho." Credit builds loyalty, but when it lives in a notebook or in your memory, some debts get forgotten, some customers disappear, and you restock using money that is sitting in other people's pockets. Salons and regulars are the biggest risk because they buy a lot and you do not want to upset them.

Quick test: add up everything customers owe you right now. If it takes more than 5 minutes, or the total shocks you, credit is eating your profit.

Sign 4

Unrecorded M-Pesa Payments: Reconcile Till, Pochi and Cash

Money arrives through the till number, your personal number, a staff member's phone and Pochi la Biashara. Cash gets counted, but M-Pesa is scattered. Some payments are sales, some are debt repayments, some are personal. Your sales record says one thing and your M-Pesa statement says another.

Quick test: compare yesterday's recorded sales with yesterday's cash plus M-Pesa. If they do not match, find out why before the gap grows.

Sign 5

Dead Stock and Expired Products: The Hidden Cost in Cosmetic Shops

Your shelves are full, yet customers keep leaving without buying. Cash is tied up in slow products, such as a cream bought in bulk "because it was cheap", while fast sellers run out. Expired items must be sold at a loss, seasonal gift sets arrive too late, and you hold 12 of the wrong foundation shade and none of the right one.

Quick test: list 5 products customers asked for this week that you did not have, then 5 that have not moved in 2 months. If both lists are easy to write, you lose money on both sides. Our Christmas stocking guide shows how to plan stock from your own sales.

Sign 6

Mixing Personal and Shop Money: Why You Cannot See Your Profit

Lunch from the till, school fees from M-Pesa, the shop's tea paid from your own phone. None of it is a crime, but when nothing is written down you cannot tell whether the shop is profitable. You only know that money is always short.

Quick test: how much did you take out of the business last month for yourself and your family? If you cannot answer within a minute, the shop and your pocket have become one account.

Sign 7

Not Knowing Your Real Profit: How to Calculate It Monthly

This sign ties the others together. After paying for stock, rent, staff, electricity and transport, how much did the shop really make last month? If the answer is "I think it was okay", you are running on feeling, not numbers, and feelings cannot tell you which product to stop buying or which leak to fix first.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my cosmetic shop busy but not making profit?

Usually because of hidden leaks: stock that disappears, selling prices that have not kept up with supplier prices, unpaid credit, and M-Pesa payments that are not recorded. Together they can wipe out your margin even when sales look good.

How do I track stock in a cosmetic shop?

Count your fastest sellers weekly, record every sale, tester, breakage and expired item, and compare your count against sales and restocking. A POS system with inventory does this automatically.

What is a good profit margin for a cosmetic shop in Kenya?

It differs by product, so work it out item by item: selling price minus buying price, divided by selling price. Check your top 10 sellers first, because they carry most of your profit.

How do I stop staff stealing in a shop?

Record every sale, limit who can change prices or delete sales, and do regular stock counts. When shortages become visible, they usually drop.

Can I use M-Pesa and a POS system together?

Yes. Record each M-Pesa payment against its sale so that cash plus M-Pesa matches your sales at the end of the day.
