How to Start a Profitable Cosmetics Business in Kenya

Selling cosmetics is easy. Making a profit is the hard part. Here are the five things that decide it, and a free calculator to check your own numbers.

On a strong day, a small cosmetic shop can sell KSh 20,000 to 50,000. At a 30 to 40% gross margin, that is roughly KSh 6,000 to 20,000 in gross profit before expenses. Treat these as an illustration, not a promise.

But here is the catch: selling cosmetics is easy. Building a profitable cosmetics business is a different game. The difference comes down to five things.

  2. 2Your suppliers

1. Your stock

A cosmetics business is bigger than a cosmetics shop

Stock is where most of your money will be sitting, so decide what you will sell before you spend your first shilling. You could specialise in:

  • HairSynthetic and human hair
  • Wigs and extensionsWigs, extensions and related products
  • Hair productsHair care and maintenance
  • Skin careProducts and treatments
  • Body careLotions, creams and body products
  • MakeupMakeup and beauty products
  • FragrancesPerfumes and body sprays
  • Beauty accessoriesAccessories and related products

Different products behave differently. Shampoo may be bought every few weeks. Hair extensions are bought when someone changes their hairstyle. A KSh 5,000 perfume may last months before the next purchase.

That does not make perfume a bad business. It means you need to understand customer frequency, not just margin. A lower-margin product that sells often can be worth more than a high-margin product that sits on the shelf for six months.

Start with the customer, not the supplier

New owners ask, "What cosmetics can I buy?" A better question is: "What cosmetics are people around me already buying?" With limited capital, do not tie your whole investment into one slow-moving category. Build a mix:

  • Fast-moving products that customers regularly need and replace.
  • Higher-margin products that leave more room between buying and selling price.
  • Impulse products, smaller items customers add to their purchase.

3 and 4. Your location and competition

Go where your customers already are, and study who is there first

Your location

  • A crowded area where many people pass by is one option.
  • Another is near a university, ladies' hostels, salons or anywhere your ideal customers already spend time.
  • Imagine your ideal customer. Where are they?
  • It is usually easier to take your business to an existing market than to bring a market to you.

Your competition

  • Find out what nearby shops charge and what they carry.
  • Pay attention to what customers ask for.
  • Can you compete on price? If not, can you offer something they do not?
  • Are there products customers want that nobody stocks?
  • Can you give a better buying experience? The same applies online.
5. Your numbers

Do the math before the business does it for you

Many owners can say, "I sold KSh 20,000 today." Great. But how much did you actually make?

That KSh 7,000 is not money you can take home. Rent, salaries, electricity, internet, marketing and delivery still come out of it. So you need to understand three different numbers:

  • RevenueHow much money came through sales.
  • Gross profitWhat remains after the cost of the products sold.
  • Net profitWhat remains after your business expenses are paid.

The bottom line

Starting a cosmetics business in Kenya is not just finding a shop, buying products and waiting for customers. Understand your market, stock what people actually want, find competitive suppliers, choose your location carefully, study the competition and keep control of your numbers.

A profitable cosmetics business is not built by selling more. It is built by understanding what you sell, what it costs, what you make, what you spend and what you keep.

Frequently asked questions

Is a cosmetics business profitable in Kenya?

It can be, but profit depends on your buying price, product mix, location and expenses, not on sales alone. As an illustration, KSh 20,000 in daily sales at a 35% gross margin is KSh 7,000 in gross profit, before rent, salaries and other expenses. Use the calculator above with your own numbers.

What is a good profit margin for a cosmetics shop?

This guide uses a 30 to 40% gross margin as an illustration. Your real margin depends on what you pay suppliers and what customers will pay, and it differs between products such as hair, skincare and fragrances. Work it out per product with the margin helper, then use your average.

How do I calculate break-even for a cosmetics shop?

Add up your monthly expenses, then divide by your gross margin and the number of days you trade. The result is the daily sales you need to cover your costs. The calculator above does it for you.

What is the difference between gross profit and net profit?

Gross profit is what remains after the cost of the products sold. Net profit is what remains after rent, salaries, electricity, internet, marketing and delivery are paid.
