Running a Hardware Shop Is More Than Selling Building Materials

From receiving cement deliveries to selling paint, electrical accessories and plumbing supplies, every product needs to be accounted for. As your business grows, keeping track of everything using notebooks and memory becomes increasingly difficult.

▤

Thousands of Products to Monitor

Hardware shops stock different brands, sizes and categories. A shop may sell cement, nails, paint, pipes, sockets, taps, adhesives and power tools. Maintaining accurate records helps you know what is available.

↗

Stock That Moves Every Day

Products leave your shelves through customer purchases throughout the day. Without proper sales records, it becomes difficult to know which items are selling and what remains in your inventory.

◷

Knowing What Needs Reordering

Running out of fast-moving products can mean lost customers and missed sales. Stock records help you identify products that need attention before they run out.

Understanding Product Profitability

Selling large quantities does not automatically mean making good profits. You need to understand buying costs, selling prices and the profit generated by your sales.

Keeping Attendants Accountable

When different employees handle customers, you need reliable records of the sales they make. Monitoring staff activity helps improve accountability.

▦

Managing Cash and M-Pesa

Customers may pay through cash or M-Pesa. Keeping records of both payment methods helps you review collections and understand your daily transactions.
