What Does a POS System Do? 5 Jobs It Does Automatically for Your Shop

If your evenings go on notebooks, calculators and M-Pesa messages, a POS can take over most of that work. Here are the 5 jobs it does for you, with what each one looks like before and after.

The short answer

A POS records every sale as it happens and updates the rest for you.

Stock, daily totals, payment types, customer credit and profit all follow from one sale entry, so you stop adding things up by hand.

See the 5 jobs

If you run a shop in Kenya, you probably spend part of every evening with a notebook, a calculator and your M-Pesa messages, trying to work out how the day went.

A POS (point of sale) system is software that records each sale as it happens and updates the rest of your shop's numbers automatically. You sell once. The system does the rest.

It is not the same as a cash register. A register only records the money. A POS also knows what you sold, what is left on the shelf, how it was paid and who still owes you.

Here are 5 jobs a POS does on its own. As you read, count how many you still do by hand.
Job 1

Updating your stock after every sale

Stock counting is the job most shop owners dread, because it takes hours and still leaves gaps.

Before

You count the shelves at the end of the week, find something missing and cannot say when it went.

After

Each sale lowers that item's stock by itself. New stock is added once when it arrives. You can see what you have without walking the shelves.

Job 2

Adding up your daily sales

Totalling the day should not take your evening.

Before

You add up the notebook at night, then try to match it with the cash in the drawer. The two rarely agree.

After

Every sale is already saved with its time, items and amount. The day's total is ready when you close, so you only check that the money matches.

Job 3

Keeping cash and M-Pesa separate

Customers pay in a mix of ways, and every payment that goes unrecorded makes your numbers less reliable.

Before

Cash and M-Pesa mix together. Some payments are never written down, so at month end you cannot say which sales the money belonged to.

After

Each sale is recorded with how it was paid. You see how much came in by cash and how much by M-Pesa, so a payment with no matching sale stands out.

Job 4

Tracking who owes you

Credit keeps regular customers loyal, but unpaid credit quietly eats your profit.

Before

Credit sits on a page in a notebook. It is easy to forget who took what and when, and old balances go unmentioned.

After

Credit sales are tied to the customer's name. You look up what they owe in seconds and follow up with the right amount.

Job 5

Showing you what you actually earn

Sales is the number everyone watches. Profit is the one that pays you.

Before

You know sales are good but cannot say which products make money and which only sit on the shelf.

After

Because the system knows what you sold and what it cost you, it shows your best sellers, your slow movers and your profit. You restock what sells and stop buying what does not.

Which Shops Benefit Most From a POS?

A POS suits any shop that sells many different items and handles cash, M-Pesa and credit. That includes:

  • Cosmetic shops, where many small products and expiry dates make stock hard to track
  • Mini supermarkets, where fast sales and thin margins make errors costly
  • Pharmacies, where stock and expiry need close attention
  • Hardware shops and bookshops, where hundreds of different items are on the shelves

You do not have to change everything at once. Most shop owners start by recording sales and stock, and the other jobs follow from there.

Frequently asked questions

What does a POS system do?

A POS records each sale as it happens and updates your stock, daily totals, payment records, customer credit and profit automatically, so you do not have to add these up by hand.

Is a POS the same as a cash register?

No. A cash register only records the money received. A POS also tracks stock, payment types, customer credit and profit.
