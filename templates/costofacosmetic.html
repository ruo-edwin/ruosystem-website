Where to buy your stock

This decision changes how much money you need to open.

Directly from manufacturers

Better prices, bigger commitment

  • Better wholesale prices, and better terms once you have a relationship
  • Minimum opening orders can reach KSh 200,000 per supplier
  • One account per brand, for example Darling KE, Angels KE, Lush KE or Sista for hair products
  • A lot of cash tied up before you know what sells

From distributors

The safer start for a new shop

  • You can start from around KSh 20,000
  • A mix of brands in one order
  • Slightly lower margin per product
  • You can test what sells before committing more money

For a new shop, a distributor is usually the safer start. You earn slightly less per product, but you avoid locking hundreds of thousands of shillings into stock before you know what your customers buy.

Where I would start with a small budget: Vision Beauty Care. Dubois Road, Excel Complex, Nairobi CBD. 0713995664

Tell them you are opening a new shop and what your budget is (KSh 20,000 or more). They help you pick products that move instead of whatever looks popular. They stock oils, treatments, shampoos, sheen sprays and other hair and beauty products.

Three decisions before you spend

  • Decide what you are known for. Skincare and haircare are repeat purchases and big parts of the market, so start there instead of stocking everything. Euromonitor puts Kenya's beauty and personal-care retail sales at KSh 124.1 billion in 2025, up 15%, with skincare the leading category.
  • Check the location before the rent. Look at who walks past: salons, offices, colleges, apartments. See what competitors sell and at what prices. A smaller shop near your customers can beat an expensive one far from them.
  • Keep cash back. If every shilling goes into stock, you have nothing left to restock the products that sell.

After you open, know what is selling

Say you open with KSh 50,000 of stock. Within a few weeks you will need answers to simple questions.

  • Which products sell fastest, and which barely move?
  • How much did I sell today, and how much stock is left?
  • What needs restocking?
  • How much came in through M-Pesa, and how much in cash?

Notebooks and manual shelf counts stop working as your product list grows. A POS answers these while you run the shop.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start a cosmetic shop in Kenya?

No single figure fits every shop. A small shop can start with carefully chosen stock from around KSh 20,000 bought through a distributor. A larger shop with a wide range can need KSh 300,000 to 500,000 or more, and opening accounts directly with several manufacturers can pass KSh 800,000 in stock alone. Rent, deposit, shelving, licences, branding, a POS and working capital come on top.

Can I start a cosmetic shop with KSh 20,000?

Yes, for a small shop. Buy from a distributor, tell them your budget, choose products with steady demand such as skincare and haircare, and keep cash for restocking. That figure covers stock only, so check that rent, fittings and licences fit your own plan.

Is it better to buy from manufacturers or distributors?

Manufacturers offer better wholesale prices, but minimum opening orders can be large. Distributors let a new shop start small and find out what sells before committing more money.

Why does a cosmetic shop need a POS system?

A POS tracks sales and stock, shows your fast and slow movers, tells you what to restock and separates M-Pesa from cash. With many products and variations, doing this by notebook gets harder as you grow. Ruo System costs KSh 2,000 per month.
