5 Promotion Ideas for Cosmetics Shops in Kenya (Bundles, Samples & WhatsApp Offers)

Dropping your prices is not the only way to get customers buying. Here are 5 promotions that bring people in and bring them back, and still leave you with a profit.

Most cosmetics shop owners in Kenya promote the same way: drop the price, put up a poster and hope. Sometimes it works. Often you sell a lot, feel busy, and find at month end that you made less than before.

A good promotion does three things. It brings in new customers, it gets existing customers to buy more or come back sooner, and it still leaves you with a profit.

The five ideas below need nothing more than a phone and the products already in your shop.

Pick one idea, try it for two weeks, then measure the result before you add the next.
Idea 1

Bundle products into a routine or a kit

Customers rarely buy one product alone. Someone buying a body lotion will probably also need soap, oil or a scrub. A bundle puts them together at a slightly lower price than buying each one separately.

  • Glow kit: lotion, body oil and scrub
  • Back-to-school kit: Vaseline, deodorant, lotion and soap
  • Hair care set: shampoo, conditioner and hair food
  • New mum kit: baby oil, baby lotion, soap and powder
  • Gift set for Christmas or Mother's Day: pack it nicely and charge for the packaging

Idea 2

Offer sample sizes and trial packs

Many customers hesitate to buy a new skincare or hair product because they are afraid of wasting money on something that does not suit them. Samples remove that fear.

  • Ask your supplier or distributor for testers, minis and sachets. Many brands give them free or cheap when you stock the full size.
  • Sell travel sizes at a low price, around KSh 100 to KSh 300. Customers who like them come back for the big bottle.
  • Give a free sample above a spend limit: "Spend KSh 1,500 and get a free mini serum." It pushes people to add one more item.
  • Use samples to move slow products. A new lotion nobody touches sells faster once people have tried a small one.

Stick to manufacturer-sealed minis and sachets. Do not decant products into unlabelled containers. It risks contamination and can damage your shop's reputation fast.

Idea 3

Run WhatsApp offers

Your customers are already on WhatsApp, so it is the cheapest place to promote. Done right, it feels like a helpful message from a shop they trust, not spam.

  • WhatsApp Status: post a new offer or arrival daily. Status disappears after 24 hours, which creates natural urgency: "Today only: Glow Kit KSh 1,500".
  • Broadcast lists: send one short offer to everyone on your list. Message only people who bought from you or asked to hear from you, and keep it to one or two a week.
  • WhatsApp Business catalogue: list your best sellers with photos and prices so customers stop asking "how much is this?"
  • "Reply to claim" offers: "Reply GLOW before Friday and get free delivery within town." You see exactly which offers work.

Ask every walk-in customer, "Can I save your number so I can tell you when new stock arrives?" That one question builds your most valuable asset: your own customer list.

Idea 4

Reward repeat customers with simple loyalty offers

It costs far more to win a new customer than to keep one you already have. A basic loyalty reward does not need an app or fancy cards.

  • Stamp card: buy 5 times, get the 6th item or a small gift free
  • Spend and earn: "Spend KSh 5,000 in a month and get 10% off your next purchase"
  • Birthday offer: a small discount in the customer's birthday month
  • Refer a friend: both the customer and the friend get something off their next purchase

The challenge is keeping track. If you cannot tell who your best customers are, how often they come or how much they spend, you cannot reward them properly. Even a simple record by phone number is a good start.

Idea 5

Use smart clearance for slow and near-expiry products

Every shop has products that sit on the shelf: the shade nobody wanted, the lotion from a supplier you will not use again, or items nearing their expiry date. That is money sitting still.

Before

An "everything on sale" poster. Your fast sellers lose margin for no reason and the slow stock still sits there.

After

A targeted clearance. Only slow movers are discounted, and your best sellers stay at full price.

  • Pair a slow item with a popular one: "Buy any hair food, get a hair clip at half price".
  • Sell near-expiry products early with a clear discount and an honest date. Never sell expired cosmetics.
  • Make a "last chance" shelf near the till so people see it as they pay.

To do this well you need to know which products are actually slow. Most owners guess. The ones who check their sales and stock every week know exactly what to discount and what to leave alone.

How To Know If a Promotion Worked

A promotion that makes the shop busy is not automatically a good one. After every offer, check four things:

  • Units sold compared with a normal week
  • Profit, not just sales
  • New customer numbers added to your WhatsApp list
  • Slow stock moved

If sales went up but profit went down, the discount was too deep. If new customers came but none returned, add a loyalty hook next time. Read more in 7 signs your cosmetic shop is busy but losing money.

Frequently asked questions

What are good promotion ideas for a cosmetics shop?

Product bundles, sample sizes and trial packs, WhatsApp offers, simple loyalty rewards and targeted clearance of slow or near-expiry stock. Each one should be checked against your margin before you launch it.

How do I price a bundle without losing money?

Add up what the items cost you, then set the bundle price above that total by the profit you want to keep. Use the bundle profit checker in this article to test your numbers.

How do I promote my cosmetics shop on WhatsApp?

Post offers on WhatsApp Status, send short messages to a broadcast list of customers who agreed to hear from you, and set up a WhatsApp Business catalogue with photos and prices.

Can I give out free samples?

Yes. Ask your suppliers for testers, minis and sachets, and give them with purchases above a set amount. Only use manufacturer-sealed samples.

How do I know which products to discount?

Look at your sales and stock records to find the items that sell slowly or are close to expiry. A POS such as Ruo System shows this from your own sales data.
