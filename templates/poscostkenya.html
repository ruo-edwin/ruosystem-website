What matters more than the monthly price?

Don't compare POS systems using the subscription alone. Compare the complete cost: software, hardware, setup, support, users, branches and any additional charges.

Cloud POS vs traditional POS: what's the difference?

One of the biggest differences between POS systems is how you pay for and maintain the software.

Cost factor Cloud POS Traditional POS
Software Usually monthly or annual subscription Often larger upfront licence/install cost
Initial cost Usually lower Usually higher
Updates Typically handled through the service May require manual upgrades or support
Data access Designed for access across supported devices Often tied more closely to the installed setup

For many small businesses, cloud POS is attractive because it reduces the amount of money required before getting started. Instead of purchasing an entire system upfront, the business pays for the software as a service.

If you already have a suitable computer, your hardware cost can be much lower. If you are starting from scratch, you may need a computer or tablet, barcode scanner and receipt printer.

Hidden POS costs businesses should ask about

A cheap monthly subscription is not necessarily the cheapest POS. Before choosing a provider, ask exactly what you will pay for and what is included.

  • Is there a setup or installation fee?
  • Are additional users charged separately?
  • Are additional branches charged separately?
  • Is training included?
  • Is customer support included?
  • Do you need to purchase the hardware separately?
  • Are there transaction or payment-processing charges?
  • What happens if you cancel the subscription?
  • Can you export your business data?
  • Does the system support the type and volume of stock your business handles?

Don't compare monthly prices alone

A KSh 1,000/month system that requires expensive add-ons may cost more than a KSh 2,000/month system that already includes the functionality you need.

Can a POS system pay for itself?

The better question is not simply "How much does the POS cost?" but "How much money can the system help me protect or manage?"

Consider a retail shop where stock discrepancies, forgotten sales, incorrect pricing or poor visibility regularly result in lost money.

Example

If better stock control and sales tracking help a business prevent even KSh 5,000 of avoidable losses in a month, a KSh 2,000 monthly POS subscription would cost less than the value it helped protect.

The actual savings will depend on the business. A POS does not automatically eliminate stock losses, but it gives the owner better records and visibility into what is being sold, received, transferred and adjusted.

For businesses dealing with hundreds or thousands of product lines, that visibility can become significantly more valuable than simply printing receipts.

What should you look for in a POS system?

Price should be one part of your decision, but the system needs to solve the actual problems in your business.

  • Inventory management — know what you have in stock.
  • Sales tracking — know what has been sold.
  • Stock movement — understand when stock was received, sold, transferred or adjusted.
  • Stock taking — compare physical stock with recorded stock.
  • Profit reporting — understand whether sales are actually generating profit.
  • Staff activity — know what actions are being performed by users.
  • Payment visibility — track the money coming through your supported payment methods.
  • Ease of use — your staff should be able to operate the system without unnecessary complexity.

This is especially important for businesses that don't just need a till, but need better control over their inventory and day-to-day operations.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a POS system cost per month in Kenya?

Cloud-based POS systems commonly fall around KSh 1,000–5,000 per month depending on the provider and features. Ruo Systems currently costs KSh 2,000 per month.

Is KSh 2,000 enough to get a complete POS system?

The software subscription is only one part of the total setup. You may also need hardware such as a computer, barcode scanner and receipt printer, depending on what you already have.

Is a cloud POS cheaper than a traditional POS?

It can be cheaper to start because cloud POS generally requires a smaller upfront software investment. The best option depends on your business requirements and the total cost over time.

Do I need a barcode scanner for a POS system?

Not necessarily. A business can operate a POS without a scanner, but scanners can make checkout faster and reduce manual product selection, especially where many products have barcodes.
